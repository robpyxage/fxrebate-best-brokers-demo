01

What is a Forex Broker?

A forex broker is the legal firm that provides a client with access to foreign-exchange trading and administers the trading account. Depending on the product and jurisdiction, the broker may act as principal or counterparty, arrange or route orders, provide prices, maintain the trading account, apply margin rules, charge trading costs and process deposits and withdrawals.

Retail forex is commonly offered as an over-the-counter (OTC) product rather than through one single global exchange. That means the client agreement, legal entity and execution arrangements matter as much as the trading software displayed on screen.

Broker ≠ platform

MetaTrader, cTrader, TradingView integration or a proprietary app is software. The broker is the legal company responsible for the account relationship. A familiar platform does not prove that the broker is authorised, financially sound or suitable.

Market size context

The BIS 2025 Triennial Survey reported average daily OTC FX turnover of about USD 9.5 trillion in April 2025 across all FX instruments. Retail broker trading is only one small part of this wider institutional market.

03

How a Forex Trade Moves Through a Broker

The exact path varies by broker and account, but the client-facing workflow can be understood in five stages.

  1. 1. Price display — the broker or platform displays bid and ask prices for the currency pair.
  2. 2. Order instruction — the trader submits a market, limit, stop or other supported order.
  3. 3. Account and risk checks — the system checks account status, margin availability, order size and product rules.
  4. 4. Execution — the order is filled under the broker’s execution arrangements. The broker may internalise exposure, route flow, or hedge some or all risk externally.
  5. 5. Account update — the fill, open position, margin usage, financing and later profit or loss are reflected in the trading account.

Important

The client sees a trading platform, but the legal and economic relationship is defined by the broker’s customer agreement and execution policy. Two brokers using the same platform can have materially different contracts and execution arrangements.

04

Currency Pairs, Bid, Ask, Spread and Pips

Forex prices are quoted in currency pairs. In EUR/USD, EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. A quote of 1.10000 means one euro is priced at 1.10000 US dollars before considering the bid/ask spread.

Simple spread example

Suppose EUR/USD is quoted at 1.10000 / 1.10020. The spread is 0.00020, or 2 pips. On a 0.10 standard-lot position (10,000 EUR), one pip is approximately USD 1 when USD is the quote currency, so crossing a 2-pip spread represents about USD 2 of spread cost before commission, financing, slippage or other charges.

Pip reminder

For most major currency pairs quoted to four traditional decimal places, one pip is 0.0001. JPY pairs are commonly treated differently. Brokers can display fractional pips, so the number of screen decimals is not the same thing as the pip definition.

05

OTC Market Structure and Price Sources

There is no single global retail forex order book that every broker must display. Wholesale FX is decentralized and OTC. Brokers can obtain prices from banks, non-bank liquidity providers, electronic venues, aggregators or internal pricing systems, and they may add mark-ups or apply product-specific pricing rules.

Because price sources and update timing differ, small differences between brokers are normal. What matters is whether the broker’s pricing and execution are consistent with its disclosed methodology and whether fills are handled fairly under the applicable rules.

Why quotes can differ

A broker’s displayed EUR/USD price may not exactly match another broker’s price at the same millisecond. Different liquidity sources, aggregation, mark-ups, latency and product structures can create small differences without implying misconduct.

Terms such as market maker, dealing desk, STP, ECN and DMA can describe aspects of an execution arrangement, but they are not a complete substitute for the broker’s legal documents. The same marketing label can be implemented differently by different firms.

There is no universal rule that makes every “ECN”, “STP”, “NDD” or “raw” account identical. Compare the actual contractual counterparty, price formation, execution policy, commission schedule and historical execution quality rather than choosing by label alone.

Where a rebate fits

An eligible cashback or rebate can reduce a defined trading cost after the broker recognises the trade under the partner arrangement. It does not improve the market price itself, remove trading risk or guarantee profitability. Eligibility depends on the broker, jurisdiction, legal entity, client type and product; some retail CFD regimes restrict or prohibit monetary inducements, including certain rebates.

Leverage, Margin, Equity and Close-Out

Leverage allows a trader to control market exposure with less initial margin. It does not make the underlying price move larger or smaller. It changes how much capital is required to support the position and how quickly losses can consume available equity.

Core concepts

ConceptSimplified explanation
Notional exposureThe economic size of the position, based on units/contracts and market price.
Required marginCollateral required to support the position under the broker’s product rules.
BalanceClosed-trade cash balance before open-position P/L.
EquityBalance adjusted for unrealized P/L and, depending on platform, other account adjustments.
Free marginEquity not currently committed as required margin.
Margin close-outBroker or regulatory mechanism that can force one or more positions to close when margin falls below a defined threshold.

Margin is not a stop-loss

Required margin is collateral supporting exposure. It is not necessarily the maximum amount that can be lost. Gaps, slippage, financing, liquidation mechanics and the legal product terms can affect outcomes. Retail protections also vary by jurisdiction and client classification.

Slippage, Gaps, Stops and Execution Risk

A stop order is an instruction, not a promise that the final fill will be exactly at the stop price. When the market moves quickly or reopens after a gap, the next executable price can be materially different from the requested level.

SituationWhat can happenWhat to review
Fast news movePrice may move through several levels before the order can be filled.Execution policy, stop handling, slippage disclosures.
Weekend / session gapThe next tradable price may open beyond a stop level.Whether guaranteed stops exist and their costs/conditions.
Large orderAvailable liquidity at the top price may be insufficient.Depth, maximum size, partial fills and average execution.
Platform / connection issueOrder instructions can be delayed, duplicated or uncertain.Order IDs, confirmations, status page, incident procedure.
Requote / rejectionSome execution models may reject or re-price an instruction.Account execution rules and market conditions in which this can happen.

Operational rule

If an order acknowledgement is missing, do not assume the trade failed. Check the account/order history or contact the broker before sending a duplicate order that could create unintended exposure.

11

Regulation and Why the Legal Entity Matters

A global broker brand can operate through several companies. The entity named in the account agreement determines which regulator, permissions, client protections and complaints routes are relevant to that account.

A company registration number or a licence number copied onto a broker website is not enough. Open the regulator’s official register independently and verify the exact legal name, status, permissions, website/contact details and any restrictions.

Clone-firm risk

Fraudsters can copy the name, licence number, logo and address of a genuine regulated company while substituting their own website, email or phone number. Compare contact details with the official regulator record, not only with the broker website.

Retail protections are jurisdiction-specific. They should never be assumed from a brand name alone. The product, legal entity and client classification determine which rules apply.

Examples, not universal rules

The UK and Australia currently apply retail CFD measures that include leverage limits, margin close-out and negative balance protection. EU national CFD intervention measures also remain relevant. Always verify the current rules for the client’s jurisdiction and exact product.

13

How to Compare Forex Brokers

A useful broker comparison is a due-diligence exercise, not a “best broker” ranking. Start with the legal relationship and protections, then compare product fit, execution and total cost.

  1. 1. Legal entity and jurisdiction — exact company, regulator, permissions and complaints route.
  2. 2. Product structure — what you are trading, who the counterparty is and whether the account is OTC/CFD/deliverable or another structure.
  3. 3. Trading cost — typical spreads, commissions, financing, conversion charges, slippage and non-trading fees.
  4. 4. Execution — order types, fill policy, platform reliability, volatility handling and execution policy.
  5. 5. Risk controls — leverage, margin close-out, negative-balance rules where applicable and stop behaviour.
  6. 6. Operations — deposits, withdrawals, verification requirements, support and service history.
  7. 7. Strategy fit — instruments, minimum size, hedging/netting, EAs/algorithms and holding/news restrictions.
  8. 8. Rebate eligibility — whether the account can be linked to the relevant partner group and whether local rules permit the rebate arrangement.

Red flags worth investigating immediately

  • • Pressure to fund quickly before the legal entity and terms can be independently verified.
  • • A website or salesperson that gives contact details different from the official regulator record.
  • • Guaranteed returns, managed-account promises or trading signals presented as nearly risk-free.
  • • Unexpected “tax”, “release”, “unlock” or insurance payments demanded before a withdrawal.
  • • Support asking for a trading password, one-time authentication code, remote-access software or API secret.
  • • Unclear withdrawal terms, unexplained account restrictions or a broker that becomes unresponsive after funding.
15

Deposits, Withdrawals and Account Security

Funding and withdrawal operations are part of broker due diligence. A broker can have competitive spreads and still be unsuitable if its payment, verification or support processes are weak or unclear.

  1. 1. Use payment methods in the same legal name as the trading account unless the broker explicitly permits otherwise.
  2. 2. Read minimum/maximum deposit and withdrawal limits, processing times and third-party fees.
  3. 3. Confirm whether profits must be withdrawn through the original funding method before alternative methods can be used.
  4. 4. Enable strong account security and protect the email account linked to the broker profile.
  5. 5. Keep broker statements, deposit receipts, withdrawal confirmations and relevant support correspondence.
  6. 6. Never share your trading password, one-time code, API secret or remote-device access with a rebate provider or unsolicited support contact.

FXRebate security reminder

FXRebate should never require your trading password, read-only password, one-time authentication code, API secret or direct control of your trading account to verify a rebate relationship. If someone asks for these credentials while claiming to represent FXRebate, do not provide them.

What the example shows

Both accounts have the same simplified monthly transaction cost in this scenario despite very different advertised spreads. The conclusion can change if typical spreads differ by session, the commission changes by volume tier, financing is added, slippage differs, or an eligible rebate is available.

Lesson

Do not choose an account by the minimum advertised spread alone. Model the all-in cost using the instruments, position sizes, holding periods and execution conditions that actually match the trading plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

YOUR QUESTIONSIs a forex broker the same as a trading platform?+

No. The platform is software; the broker is the legal firm responsible for the account relationship and execution terms.

YOUR QUESTIONSDoes “ECN” automatically mean the broker is better?+

No. ECN can describe a form of connectivity or pricing access, but regulation, legal counterparty, total cost, execution quality and operational reliability still need to be checked.

YOUR QUESTIONSWhy do two brokers show slightly different forex prices?+

Retail FX is OTC. Brokers can use different liquidity sources, mark-ups, aggregation methods and update timing, so small quote differences are normal.

YOUR QUESTIONSIs the lowest spread always the cheapest account?+

No. Commission, financing, slippage, conversion charges and other fees can make the all-in cost higher.

YOUR QUESTIONSDoes higher leverage change profit or loss on the same market exposure?+

No. For identical exposure and the same price move, gross P/L is the same. Higher leverage reduces the margin required to support that exposure and can therefore make it easier to take excessive risk.

YOUR QUESTIONSDoes regulation mean a broker cannot fail?+

No. Regulation can impose standards, supervision and client protections, but it cannot eliminate business, market, operational or fraud risk.

YOUR QUESTIONSCan an existing account be moved under FXRebate?+

That depends on the broker and the existing partner relationship. Some brokers permit an IB/partner transfer; others require approval from the current IB or a new account.

Knowledge Quiz

Think about each question, then reveal the answer to check your understanding.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK1. A broker offers MetaTrader 5. Does that prove the broker is regulated?+

Answer: No. The software and the legal broker are separate.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK2. EUR/USD is 1.10000 / 1.10020. What is the spread?+

Answer: 2 pips.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK3. What is the best way to compare a “raw” account with a spread-only account?+

Answer: Compare the all-in cost for the way you actually trade, including spread, commission, financing, slippage and other relevant charges.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK4. If two positions have identical market exposure, does higher leverage change the gross P/L from the same price move?+

Answer: No. Leverage changes margin required, not the price movement on identical exposure.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK5. What should be verified first when a broker brand has several companies?+

Answer: The exact legal entity named in the account agreement and its current regulatory permissions.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK6. Why can two brokers show slightly different quotes?+

Answer: The OTC market uses multiple liquidity sources, pricing methods, mark-ups and update timing.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK7. Does a stop order guarantee the stop price?+

Answer: Not necessarily. Gaps, fast markets and liquidity can cause slippage unless a specific guaranteed-stop feature applies.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK8. What does negative balance protection do where it applies?+

Answer: It limits the client’s aggregate liability under the applicable rule/account structure, but the exact protection is jurisdiction- and product-specific.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK9. Should FXRebate need your trading password to verify a rebate account?+

Answer: No.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK10. Is the highest advertised rebate automatically the best broker choice?+

Answer: No. Legal safety, execution, all-in cost, product fit and rebate eligibility all need to be considered together.

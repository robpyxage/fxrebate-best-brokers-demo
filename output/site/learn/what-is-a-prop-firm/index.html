01

What Is a Prop Firm?

A proprietary trading firm, or “prop firm,” is a business that seeks to earn money from trading financial markets using capital associated with the firm rather than simply executing customer orders for a commission. In the traditional model, the firm selects traders, allocates risk capital, controls exposure and shares trading profits through employment or contractual compensation arrangements.

In the modern retail market, “prop firm” often refers to a different business model: a trader buys access to an evaluation, challenge or subscription, trades under a defined set of risk rules, and may become eligible for a reward or payout if the required conditions are met. Many of these stages are simulated, and some firms may later move selected traders to live capital.

Important distinction

The phrase “funded account” is a marketing label, not a universal legal definition. Always verify whether the account is simulated, live, or part of a hybrid model, and who the contracting party is.

03

How a Retail Prop-Firm Evaluation Usually Works

  1. 1. Choose a programme — account size, asset class, platform, evaluation structure and fee.
  2. 2. Accept the rulebook — profit target, maximum loss, daily loss, trading days, consistency rules and prohibited practices.
  3. 3. Trade the evaluation — commonly in a simulated environment with market-based quotes.
  4. 4. Pass all required objectives — usually with open positions closed and no rule violation.
  5. 5. Enter the funded stage — which may still be simulated and can have different rules from the evaluation.
  6. 6. Become eligible for a payout or reward — only after the programme’s contractual conditions are satisfied.
  7. 7. Potential live allocation — some firms may move selected traders to live capital; others remain entirely simulated.

Rule hierarchy

The evaluation rules, funded-stage rules, payout rules and prohibited-strategy rules should be read separately. Passing the challenge does not mean the same rules continue unchanged.

04

Simulated Accounts, Live Accounts and “Funded” Accounts

A simulated account uses fictitious or virtual capital while attempting to reproduce market conditions. The trader’s orders do not necessarily reach a live exchange, broker or liquidity provider. A live account, by contrast, places real orders or positions into a live market environment through a broker, futures commission merchant, exchange member or other execution arrangement.

Some providers explicitly state that all client accounts are simulated; some offer a simulated funded stage before any live allocation; and some futures programmes may move selected traders to a live account after demonstrating consistent performance.

05

Headline Account Size vs Real Risk Budget

One of the most important concepts in prop trading is that the advertised account size is not the amount a trader can lose. A “$100,000 account” may have a maximum-loss rule of only a fraction of that amount, and a separate daily-loss limit may be smaller still.

Risk principle

Position sizing should be based on the current permitted loss buffer and the strategy’s stop distance — not on the headline account size.

06

The Main Drawdown and Loss-Limit Models

Prop-firm rules often use the word “drawdown,” but the calculation method matters more than the label. A trader should identify the reference value, reset time, whether unrealized P&L counts, and whether the threshold can move.

Rule typeTypical mechanismQuestions to ask
Static maximum lossA fixed equity or balance floor.Does open P&L count? Do commissions/swaps count?
Daily lossLoss allowance resets at a defined session or clock time.What balance/equity snapshot is used? What timezone?
End-of-day trailingFloor rises based on a daily high-water mark or closing balance.When exactly does it update? Does it stop trailing?
Intraday trailingThreshold can move during the session as equity/balance reaches new highs.Does unrealized profit raise the floor? Can it lock?

07

Worked Example: Daily Loss Can Reset Against You

Assume a hypothetical $100,000 simulated account with a $5,000 daily-loss amount. The programme recalculates the daily-loss floor at midnight from the account balance. At midnight the balance is $102,000, so the new floor becomes $97,000.

If the trader is holding an open position with a $3,000 unrealized loss shortly after the reset, account equity would be $99,000. Only $2,000 of additional equity loss remains before the $97,000 floor is reached — even though the trader may still think of the account as a “$100,000 account.”

Lesson

Reset mechanics can change the available risk overnight. Before carrying positions across a reset time, recalculate the next session’s permitted equity floor.

Profit Targets, Minimum Days and Consistency Rules

Passing an evaluation usually requires more than simply making money. Programmes can include a profit target, a minimum number of trading days, a best-day or consistency requirement, and conditions about when the target is considered achieved.

Scaling planControls maximum size as the account develops.Trading the marketing maximum may violate stage-specific limits.

Position Sizing Under Prop-Firm Constraints

A disciplined prop trader typically works backward from the rule limits. The goal is not to use the maximum leverage or contract size; it is to keep one trade, one day and one correlated theme small enough that normal losses do not threaten the programme.

Simple sizing framework

  1. 1. Calculate the current distance to the hard maximum-loss threshold.
  2. 2. Calculate the remaining daily-loss allowance after realized and unrealized P&L, fees and commissions.
  3. 3. Choose a smaller internal risk budget than either hard limit.
  4. 4. Divide that internal risk budget by the stop distance and instrument value per point/pip/tick.
  5. 5. Check correlated positions and total exposure before submitting the order.
  6. 6. Recalculate after large profits, withdrawals, resets or a change in the programme stage.

Payouts, Rewards and Profit Splits

A payout is not automatically the same thing as withdrawing profit from a conventional brokerage account. In a simulated-funded model, the payment may be a contractual reward calculated from simulated performance. In a live model, compensation may relate to actual trading profits under the firm’s agreement.

TermWhat to verify
Profit split / reward splitPercentage, calculation base, caps and whether it changes over time.
Payout frequencyFirst eligible date, waiting period and later cadence.
Minimum profitable daysWhether a certain number of positive sessions is required.
Consistency / best-day conditionWhether payout eligibility uses a separate consistency formula.
Buffer requirementWhether profits must remain in the account after a payout request.
Effect of withdrawalWhether the loss floor, trailing threshold or account balance resets.
Compliance reviewWhether payouts can be delayed or denied after a rule/practice review.

Trading Restrictions and Prohibited Practices

Prop firms can permit a wide range of strategies while still prohibiting behaviour that exploits a simulator, violates exchange rules, depends on unrealistic fills, or circumvents risk controls. Restrictions differ by provider and can also change between evaluation and funded stages.

AreaQuestions to verify before trading
News tradingCan positions be opened or closed around scheduled news?
Overnight / weekend holdingMust positions be flat at a daily close or before weekends?
Expert Advisors / algorithmsAre EAs or automated strategies permitted? Any request or speed limits?
Trade copiersCan you copy between your own accounts? Are third-party signals prohibited?
Hedging across accountsCan opposite positions be held across multiple accounts/providers?
High-frequency / latency strategiesAre stale-price or latency-based strategies prohibited?
Account sharingCan anyone else access or trade the account?
Maximum position sizeDo contract/lot caps or scaling plans apply?

Regulation, Brokers, FCMs and Counterparties

A retail prop-firm programme may not itself be a brokerage account. If the provider says a trader will receive live futures, forex or derivative market access, identify the separate regulated intermediary involved and verify that intermediary directly with the relevant official register.

In the United States, the CFTC advises traders to check registration and disciplinary history through NFA BASIC before working with firms or persons that must be registered for futures, options, forex or derivatives activity. In the United Kingdom, the FCA warns consumers to verify the exact firm and contact details because clone firms can copy genuine company names or reference numbers.

Do not infer regulation

A company registration, business address, payment processor, platform integration, or relationship with a broker does not by itself prove that every service offered by a prop programme is regulated or protected in the same way as a brokerage account.

17

Red Flags and Marketing Claims to Examine Carefully

  • • “$100,000 funded account” presented without explaining the actual loss limit or whether the account is simulated.
  • • “Guaranteed payout” or “guaranteed funding” language that ignores rule compliance and contractual review.
  • • Rule pages that conflict with terms and conditions or are difficult to date/version.
  • • Unclear legal entity, missing company details, or payments sent to a different unexplained entity.
  • • Pressure to buy immediately because a discount is “ending” while important terms remain unclear.
  • • Unclear distinction between evaluation, funded-simulated and live accounts.
  • • Rules that can be changed retroactively without clear notice or version history.
  • • Payout denials with no documented appeal or review process.
  • • Requests for trading passwords, remote-device access, crypto seed phrases or one-time authentication codes.

Frequently Asked Questions

YOUR QUESTIONSDoes passing a prop-firm challenge mean I receive $100,000 in cash?+

No. The quoted account size is a trading parameter, not a cash payment. The actual usable loss budget can be much smaller, and the funded stage may be simulated.

YOUR QUESTIONSAre all funded accounts live?+

No. Some providers explicitly use simulated funded accounts and pay contractual rewards based on simulated performance. Some offer a path to live capital; the exact model must be checked.

YOUR QUESTIONSWhat is the most important rule to understand?+

The hardest risk limit: how the maximum loss or trailing threshold is calculated, when it resets or moves, and whether unrealized P&L and fees count. A strategy can be profitable overall and still fail the programme by touching that threshold.

YOUR QUESTIONSCan a profitable trade still be a rule violation?+

Yes. A trade can make money but still breach a rule about news, position size, account copying, holding times, prohibited technology or other programme conditions.

YOUR QUESTIONSIs a prop firm the same as a broker?+

No. A prop programme may use broker-like software, but its primary relationship is usually an evaluation or trading-performance agreement. Any broker, futures commission merchant or execution venue should be identified and assessed separately.

YOUR QUESTIONSWhy can payout rules matter more than the profit split?+

A high advertised split is not useful if a trader cannot meet the qualifying days, consistency limit, minimum buffer, payout cap or review conditions. Compare the entire payout process, not only the percentage.

Knowledge Quiz

Think about each question, then reveal the answer to check your understanding.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK1. Does a “$100,000 funded account” mean the trader can lose $100,000?+

Answer: No. The real loss budget is determined by maximum-loss, daily-loss and other programme rules.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK2. What is the key difference between a simulated funded account and a live account?+

Answer: A simulated account uses fictitious capital and orders do not necessarily reach a live market; a live account uses real capital and live-market execution.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK3. Why is a trailing drawdown different from a static drawdown?+

Answer: A trailing threshold can rise as performance reaches new highs, while a static floor remains fixed.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK4. What information is required to calculate a daily-loss rule correctly?+

Answer: The loss amount, reference balance/equity, reset time/timezone, treatment of unrealized P&L and fees, and any stage-specific details.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK5. Why can a large winning day create a problem?+

Answer: A consistency or best-day rule may limit how much of total profit can come from one day.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK6. Name four costs besides the evaluation fee.+

Answer: Examples: resets, activation fees, market data, platform fees, commissions, exchange fees or payout-related costs.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK7. Is a high profit split enough to compare two firms?+

Answer: No. Payout eligibility, caps, buffers, rules, costs and account type also matter.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK8. Why should the exact legal entity be recorded?+

Answer: The contracting party determines which terms apply and helps distinguish the programme from brokers or other counterparties.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK9. Can a provider prohibit a strategy even if it is profitable?+

Answer: Yes. Firms can prohibit simulator exploitation, hedging across accounts, certain copiers, latency strategies or other conduct under their terms.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK10. What should be saved before starting an evaluation?+

Answer: The applicable terms, rule pages, payout policy, prohibited-practices policy, fees, programme name and the date/version reviewed.

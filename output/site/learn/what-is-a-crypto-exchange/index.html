01

What is a Crypto Exchange?

A crypto exchange is a service or trading venue that allows users to buy, sell or exchange crypto assets. Depending on the platform, users may trade crypto against fiat currencies such as EUR or USD, stablecoins such as USDT or USDC, or other crypto assets.

The term “exchange” can describe very different structures. A centralized exchange (CEX) is operated by a company that maintains client accounts and typically controls the trading system. A decentralized exchange (DEX) is usually a blockchain-based protocol where users connect a wallet and interact with smart contracts instead of opening a conventional custodial account.

Important distinction

A crypto exchange is not the blockchain itself. The exchange may keep an internal record of client balances and only interact with the blockchain when deposits and withdrawals occur.

02

Centralized Exchanges (CEX) and Decentralized Exchanges (DEX)

The first major distinction is whether the venue is operated by a centralized intermediary or by a protocol that users access with their own wallets.

CustodyThe platform may hold client crypto or control private keys.Users generally keep control of their wallet keys.
ExecutionOften an internal order book or matching engine.Often automated market makers, pools, aggregators or on-chain order books.
Fiat accessOften supports cards, bank transfers or other fiat rails.Usually crypto-to-crypto; fiat access may require a separate provider.
Main operational riskExchange failure, hacking, withdrawal restrictions, account access.Smart-contract, wallet, token, bridge and network risks.
Transaction visibilityMany trades happen off-chain inside the exchange ledger.Transactions or settlements are generally visible on-chain.

03

How a Trade Moves Through a Centralized Crypto Exchange

A simplified trading workflow can be understood in six stages. The exact sequence depends on the exchange, product and order type.

  1. 1. Account and funding — the user deposits fiat or crypto and the exchange credits the account after the required checks or blockchain confirmations.
  2. 2. Price discovery — the trading interface displays the current order book or an exchange-generated conversion quote.
  3. 3. Order instruction — the user submits a market, limit, stop or other supported order.
  4. 4. Matching and execution — the system matches the order against available liquidity or executes it under the platform’s conversion model.
  5. 5. Internal balance update — the account ledger changes to reflect the acquired and sold assets plus applicable fees.
  6. 6. Withdrawal — if the user sends crypto to an external wallet, the exchange initiates an on-chain transaction on the selected network.

Why this matters

A trade executed inside a centralized exchange is not necessarily an on-chain transaction. This is why trading can be fast even when the underlying blockchain has slower settlement times.

04

Trading Pairs, Order Books, Bid, Ask and Spread

Crypto markets are quoted in pairs. In BTC/USDT, BTC is the base asset and USDT is the quote asset. A price of 68,000 means one bitcoin is valued at 68,000 USDT in that market.

An order book lists available buy and sell orders at different prices. The highest available bid is the best price at which a buyer is currently willing to buy; the lowest available ask is the best price at which a seller is willing to sell. The difference is the spread.

Common order types

Order typePurposeKey consideration
MarketExecute immediately against available liquidity.Final average price may differ from the top-of-book quote.
LimitTrade only at the chosen price or better.Execution is not guaranteed.
Stop / triggerActivate an order after a trigger price is reached.Trigger and execution price can differ in fast markets.
Post-onlyAttempt to add liquidity rather than immediately take it.May be rejected or repriced depending on exchange rules.
IOC / FOKControl how much of an order may remain unfilled.Availability and definitions vary by venue.

05

Liquidity and Slippage

Liquidity describes how easily a trade can be executed without causing a large price change. A market can show an attractive last price but still have limited quantity available near that price.

Slippage occurs when the average execution price differs from the expected price. It can increase when order size is large relative to the order book, when volatility rises, or when the market is thin.

Example

If only 0.10 BTC is offered at 68,005 but a trader sends a market order to buy 1 BTC, the remaining 0.90 BTC must be filled at higher ask prices if available. The average fill can therefore be above 68,005.

The all-in cost of using an exchange can include more than the headline trading commission. Traders should distinguish costs that apply to the trade itself from costs that apply to funding, borrowing or moving assets.

Leverage warning

Leverage can cause positions to be liquidated after a relatively small adverse price move. The relevant figures are not only the advertised leverage ratio, but also maintenance margin, liquidation methodology and fees.

08

Custody, Wallets and Private Keys

Custody is one of the most important differences between traditional brokerage and crypto markets. Crypto assets are controlled through private keys. A wallet stores or manages those keys; the assets themselves remain recorded on the relevant blockchain.

Hot wallets and cold storage

Hot wallets are connected to online systems and are convenient for withdrawals and daily operations. Cold storage keeps keys offline or behind stronger operational controls. Exchanges may use a combination of both. Users should not assume that a “cold storage” claim by itself explains how client assets are legally held or protected.

Security notice

FXRebate will never need your private keys, seed phrase, exchange password, two-factor authentication code or direct access to a crypto wallet. Do not provide these credentials to anyone claiming they are required for cashback, account verification or support.

09

Deposits, Withdrawals and Blockchain Networks

Withdrawing crypto requires more care than a bank transfer because the same asset can exist on multiple networks. Sending to an incompatible address or choosing the wrong network can cause permanent loss or require a difficult recovery process.

  1. 1. Confirm the exact asset — for example, USDT is issued on several networks.
  2. 2. Confirm the network supported by both the sending exchange and the receiving wallet or exchange.
  3. 3. Check the destination address carefully and include a memo/tag when required.
  4. 4. Review the withdrawal fee, minimum withdrawal and expected network confirmations.
  5. 5. For a new destination, consider testing with a small amount before sending a large balance.
  6. 6. Keep transaction IDs and account records for reconciliation and support.

A blockchain transaction is generally irreversible once confirmed. Customer support may be unable to recover assets sent to an unsupported address or network.

Regulation, Authorisation and the Legal Entity

Crypto regulation differs by jurisdiction and can change quickly. The relevant question is not simply whether an exchange says it is “regulated,” but which legal entity provides the service, what that entity is authorised or registered to do, and what protections apply to the specific product.

In the European Union, the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) establishes a framework for certain crypto-assets and crypto-asset service providers. EU authorities have nevertheless warned that crypto assets remain risky and that protections can be limited, especially when providers are unauthorised or services fall outside the relevant framework.

In the United Kingdom, certain cryptoasset businesses fall within FCA registration requirements under anti-money-laundering rules. Registration under one regime should not be confused with the full protections that can apply to traditional investment products or bank deposits.

Verify independently

Open the official regulator or competent-authority register yourself. Confirm the exact legal name and website. Scam sites frequently copy genuine company names, registration numbers and logos.

12

Proof of Reserves: Useful Evidence, Not a Complete Solvency Test

Some exchanges publish proof-of-reserves reports or cryptographic evidence intended to show that certain on-chain assets exist and correspond to customer balances. This can improve transparency, but it does not necessarily provide a complete picture of the company’s financial condition.

A reserve snapshot may not fully show liabilities, off-chain obligations, related-party exposures, asset encumbrances, operational controls or the financial position of every legal entity in a group. U.S. investor guidance has specifically cautioned that proof-of-reserves reports are not equivalent to audited financial statements.

Practical interpretation

Treat proof of reserves as one data point. Combine it with legal-entity verification, custody disclosures, financial information where available, withdrawal history, security controls and the rights described in the customer agreement.

13

How to Compare Crypto Exchanges

A useful exchange comparison is a due-diligence exercise, not a “best exchange” ranking. Start with legal safety and custody, then assess trading fit and cost.

  1. 1. Legal entity and jurisdiction — exact company, authorisation/registration, restricted countries and complaints route.
  2. 2. Custody and ownership — who controls keys, how client assets are held and what the agreement says in insolvency.
  3. 3. Security controls — 2FA, withdrawal allow-lists, API permissions, session history and anti-phishing tools.
  4. 4. Trading markets — available assets, fiat pairs, stablecoin pairs, spot, margin and derivatives.
  5. 5. Liquidity and execution — order-book depth, typical spread, order types and platform reliability.
  6. 6. Fees — maker/taker, withdrawal, network, fiat funding, conversion, borrowing and funding rates.
  7. 7. Operations — deposit and withdrawal processing, verification requirements, service availability and support.
  8. 8. Records and tax data — statements, downloadable trade history and transaction identifiers.

“Deep liquidity”What is the visible depth at my intended order size and market?
“Institutional security”Which concrete controls, custody arrangements and audit reports support that claim?
“Proof of reserves”What assets and liabilities are included, who performed the work, and what period does it cover?
“Fully regulated”Which legal entity is authorised, by which authority, for which services?
“Guaranteed yield”What creates the yield, who is the borrower/counterparty, and can principal be lost?

Red flags worth investigating immediately

  • • Guaranteed returns, risk-free trading or pressure to deposit quickly.
  • • Support staff requesting passwords, seed phrases, remote-access software or 2FA codes.
  • • Withdrawal demands requiring an unexpected “tax,” “unlock fee” or additional deposit paid to a private wallet.
  • • A website or app whose legal company cannot be verified independently.
  • • Contact details that differ from those on the official regulator or company website.
  • • Large balances shown on a platform that cannot be independently withdrawn.

Crypto Exchanges vs Forex Brokers

Both can provide trading interfaces, but the underlying product, custody and market structure can be very different.

TopicCrypto exchangeRetail forex / CFD broker
Underlying marketCrypto assets and/or crypto derivatives.Currencies and/or OTC derivatives such as CFDs.
CustodyCan involve holding actual crypto and private-key infrastructure.Usually account balances and contractual positions rather than blockchain keys.
SettlementMay be internal until an on-chain withdrawal occurs.Usually reflected in broker account records; no blockchain settlement.

CostsMaker/taker, spread, slippage, network, funding/borrow.Spread, commission, financing/swap, slippage, conversion.

Practical Case Study: Which Exchange Is Cheaper?

A trader wants to buy USD 10,000 equivalent of a liquid crypto asset and later sell the same amount. Two fictional exchanges advertise different fee structures.

InputExchange AExchange B
Round-trip trading fee0.20% total0.10% total
Estimated spread + slippage0.08%0.18%
Crypto withdrawal feeUSD 8 equivalentUSD 20 equivalent
Network fee paid separatelyUSD 3USD 3

Step-by-step

Exchange A: trading fee = USD 20; estimated spread/slippage = USD 8; withdrawal = USD 8; network = USD 3. Estimated total = USD 39.

Exchange B: trading fee = USD 10; estimated spread/slippage = USD 18; withdrawal = USD 20; network = USD 3. Estimated total = USD 51.

Lesson

The lower advertised trading fee does not necessarily produce the lower all-in cost. The result changes with order size, market depth, fee tier, number of withdrawals, chosen network and execution conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

YOUR QUESTIONSIs a crypto exchange the same as a crypto wallet?+

No. An exchange is a service or venue for trading and related account functions. A wallet is software or hardware used to manage cryptographic keys. Some exchanges provide custodial wallet functionality inside the account.

YOUR QUESTIONSDo I own the crypto shown in my exchange account?+

That depends on the legal agreement and custody model. The economic balance shown in an account does not by itself explain the user’s legal rights if the platform fails. Read the custody and ownership provisions carefully.

YOUR QUESTIONSWhy can the same crypto have different prices on different exchanges?+

Each venue has its own order flow, liquidity, participant base, fees and operational constraints. Arbitrage tends to link prices, but they do not have to be identical at every moment.

YOUR QUESTIONSIs “proof of reserves” the same as an audit?+

No. A reserve attestation or cryptographic proof can be useful, but it may not provide the same scope or assurance as audited financial statements and does not automatically prove solvency.

YOUR QUESTIONSAre exchange withdrawals always on-chain?+

External crypto withdrawals normally require a blockchain transaction. Transfers between users inside the same platform may be recorded only on the exchange’s internal ledger, depending on the service.

YOUR QUESTIONSDoes regulation eliminate crypto risk?+

No. Regulation can impose rules and provide certain protections, but crypto assets remain volatile and operational, custody and insolvency risks can still exist. The applicable protection depends on the product and legal entity.

Knowledge Quiz

Think about each question, then reveal the answer to check your understanding.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK1. What is the main difference between a CEX and a DEX?+

Answer: A CEX is operated by a centralized company and often maintains client accounts; a DEX typically uses smart contracts and user-connected wallets.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK2. In BTC/USDT, what is the quote asset?+

Answer: USDT.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK3. Why can a market order experience slippage?+

Answer: Because the available liquidity at the best displayed price may be insufficient, so the order fills across multiple price levels.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK4. Name four possible crypto-exchange costs besides the headline trading fee.+

Answer: Examples: spread, slippage, withdrawal fee, network fee, fiat funding fee, conversion markup, borrowing cost or perpetual funding.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK5. What does “not your keys, not your coins” try to highlight?+

Answer: When a third party controls private keys, the user depends on that custodian for access and recovery.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK6. Why must the blockchain network be checked before withdrawing?+

Answer: The same token can exist on multiple networks; using an incompatible network or address can cause loss.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK7. Does proof of reserves prove that an exchange has no liabilities?+

Answer: No. It may show certain reserves but can omit liabilities or other financial risks.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK8. What should be verified before relying on an exchange’s claim that it is regulated?+

Answer: The exact legal entity, official register entry, permissions and the services/products covered.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK9. What security information should never be given to FXRebate or anyone else?+

Answer: Private keys, seed phrases, exchange passwords and 2FA/authentication codes.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK10. Why is the cheapest advertised fee not always the cheapest exchange?+

Answer: Total cost can also include spread, slippage, funding, conversion and withdrawal/network costs.

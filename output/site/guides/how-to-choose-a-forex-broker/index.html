The right broker should meet your requirements for client protection, trading conditions and service. Start by checking the company behind your account, then compare the full cost of trading. Use the examples and checklist below to make your shortlist.

01

Verify who will hold your account

Find the exact legal entity in the client agreement. Check its licence status and relevant permissions on the regulator’s official register, and match the website and contact details. Confirm that it can serve clients in your country. A licence belonging to another company in the same group does not automatically cover your account.

02

Understand the protections that apply

Ask how client funds are held and whether they are separated from the broker’s own money. Check whether negative balance protection applies, and which independent complaints or compensation arrangements you can access. These protections vary by entity, jurisdiction and client classification; compensation schemes do not cover ordinary trading losses.

03

Check whether the account suits your strategy

Compare the instruments you actually trade, minimum trade sizes, supported platforms and rules for automated trading, scalping or hedging. Understand margin requirements and the stop-out level at which positions may be closed. Higher leverage increases both potential gains and losses; it should not be your main reason for choosing a broker.

04

Compare the full cost of trading

A headline spread is only one part of the price. Compare the same instrument, trade size, account currency and holding period. Check whether a quoted commission is charged per side or for opening and closing a trade together.

06

Account for execution and service

Slippage is the difference between the requested and executed price and can improve or worsen your result. Review the execution policy and order restrictions. Ask support about withdrawal methods, verification requirements, fees and processing times. If you proceed, start small and test a withdrawal early; one successful withdrawal is not a guarantee of future reliability.

07

Build your broker shortlist

08

Before opening or linking an account

Watch for warning signs: Avoid guaranteed-return promises and pressure to deposit immediately. Reviews can reveal recurring complaints, but awards, broker age and star ratings do not establish reliability. Do not let a low price compensate for an unresolved regulatory concern.
09

Compare available rebates with FXRebate

Once a broker meets your requirements, compare its FXRebate offer. Check eligible accounts and instruments, how rebates are calculated and when they are paid. Follow the relevant instructions for opening a new account or transferring an existing account to the FXRebate partner group. Confirm eligibility before expecting rebates.

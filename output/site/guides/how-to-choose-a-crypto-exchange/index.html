Choose an exchange by checking who operates it, how your assets are held and what buying, selling and withdrawing will cost. This guide focuses on centralised exchanges, with comparison tools to help you assess the services you actually need.

01

Verify the exchange and its legal entity

Identify the company serving your account and check its status and permitted services on the relevant regulator’s official register. For EU clients, check ESMA’s MiCA register and the national regulator. Match the official website and confirm availability in your country. Authorisation does not make every listed token safe or cover every product offered under the same brand.

02

Understand custody and client protection

On a custodial exchange, the provider controls the private keys for assets held on your behalf. Read how customer assets are separated, whether they may be lent or reused, and what happens if the exchange fails. Do not assume crypto balances have bank deposit protection or an investor compensation guarantee.

Self-custody gives you control of your keys but also responsibility for securing and recovering access. Losing a recovery phrase or making an incorrect transfer can result in permanent loss.

03

Look for meaningful security controls

Check for passkeys or strong two-factor authentication, withdrawal address allowlisting, login alerts and controls on API access. Review the exchange’s incident history and response to past problems. Enable the available protections before funding your account, and never share recovery phrases or verification codes with support.

Proof of reserves has limits: A reserves report can offer useful information, but it is not a full financial statement audit and may not capture all liabilities. It does not guarantee solvency or protect you against future losses.

04

Compare fees and execution

Compare the same trading pair, order size and purchase method. Instant-buy, card purchase and order-book trading can have different pricing. Maker orders add liquidity to the order book; taker orders execute against available liquidity. A limit order is not automatically a maker order.

06

Check liquidity on your chosen pair

Look at the order book around the current price and the spread for the pair you intend to trade. Exchange-wide volume alone does not show whether your order can be filled at a reasonable price. Slippage can increase for large orders or during volatile markets; limit orders control your price but may not fill.

07

Treat additional products separately

Earn may involve lending or other strategies, while staking has its own network and provider risks. Check variable yields, lockups and potential penalties or losses. Neither is equivalent to a guaranteed savings account. Leveraged products can liquidate positions and require a separate assessment of fees and risk.

09

Before making your first transfer

Test support and withdrawals: Ask a specific question before depositing and review support hours. Check for withdrawal holds or network suspensions. A successful test is useful but does not guarantee future access. Avoid guaranteed-return claims and pressure to transfer funds urgently.

10

Compare available rebates with FXRebate

For exchanges with an FXRebate offer, check eligible products, fee tiers and the required referral or account-linking process. Confirm whether existing accounts qualify, which fees earn rebates, and the payment currency and schedule. Compare costs after all applicable discounts; do not assume trading rebates cover withdrawal fees or Earn and staking products.
